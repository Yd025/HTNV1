What we built, and how it works

  1. Build the map the cameras actually have to see

    We exported the Fort Ross terrain, water mask, camera field of view, mounting geometry and rendering limits from ArcticSim assets. A 49 × 49 planning grid represents water routes and legal land sites. Terrain can block a camera's view.

  2. Learn simple boat statistics

    We generated 256 boat routes with random starts on connected water cells. Smoothed frequency counts learned starting-cell probabilities and 15,360 movement transitions. The starting-location prior helps propose tower positions; the mission controller does not receive the hidden boat route.

  3. Try 12 tower placements

    We kept the default pair as a reference, proposed a pair using likely boat locations and camera visibility, and explored alternatives by moving promising sites and occasionally sampling across the map. Sites snap to valid land, with at least 250 metres between towers. Every pair faces the same 24 training missions.

  4. Select on separate validation missions

    The default candidate and the three strongest training candidates each faced 24 new missions. Scoring prioritizes true tower acquisition with a penalty for false confirmations; secondary criteria include aircraft handoff, tracking continuity, delay, position error and distance. Candidate 8 won this validation comparison. Its positions were frozen before testing.

  5. Confirm, dispatch, and follow observations

    Fixed towers scan at 6° per second. Two consistent fresh tower observations within 15 seconds confirm a contact. The quadcopter approaches a useful viewing distance and the fixed-wing supports forward observation and reacquisition. Two separate accepted observations from one aircraft confirm the handoff. Tracking can coast through gaps, but expires after 45 seconds without accepted observations.

  6. Test on ships the selection never saw

    We evaluated the frozen pair and two reference policies on the same 200 new random missions, including clear, haze and glare conditions. Every miss remains in the results. Ground-truth positions are used to simulate observations and score estimates, never as inputs to the mission controller.

What “trained” means here

This is statistical motion learning plus a bounded search over tower locations.

  • Learned: starting-water-cell probabilities and movement transitions.
  • Optimized: two tower positions and initial headings.
  • Programmed: camera response assumptions, confirmation rules, target filtering, aircraft behavior and scoring.
  • Separate work: training a real image detector on labeled ship images and measuring its performance on camera footage.

The saved movement transitions are not evidence of a trained neural flight policy. The current placement proposal uses the starting-location prior; the saved legacy weight values are retained for compatibility and are not optimized pursuit gains.

Scenario and sensor assumptions

Boats move at 3 m/s between neighboring safe water nodes. The simulated vessel is 6 m long. Camera observations depend on line of sight, field of view, apparent image size, distance and synthetic weather/contrast; noise and clutter are injected.

Tower camera field of view is about 60° horizontally. The 1,500 m far clip limits rendering; it is not a verified recognition range. The quad camera is fixed around 20° downward and the plane camera around 8° downward.

How accurate is it?

The main result is five-minute simulated detection success. It tells us how often the towers confirmed the correct ship during a mission.

A successful detection needs two tower observations and an estimated position within 150 m of the ship's true position. That 150 m is a scoring tolerance, not a guarantee of localization accuracy.

Detection improved; tracking continuity did not. Across the full test set, selected towers raised detection from 29% to 40% and aircraft handoff from 26.5% to 31%. But aircraft custody after both towers lost sight fell from 58.6% to 24.4% of eligible samples. Those eligible samples differ between placements, so this is a conditional continuity measure, not a paired all-mission score.

Position error

The selected configuration has 323.2 m pooled RMSE across available observed and predicted track estimates. RMSE is the square root of mean squared position error. Missing estimates are not included, so this number alone cannot describe overall reliability.

Time to confirmation

Average delay is 222.9 seconds when every miss is assigned the 300-second deadline. The reference is 244.2 seconds. The 21.3-second improvement has a paired bootstrap 95% interval of 0.59 to 41.93 seconds. This is not the average delay only among successful detections.

False contacts and missed ships

There were 2 false confirmed cues in the selected policy's 200 missions, versus 3 with default sites and the same controller. Meanwhile, 120 ships were not correctly confirmed within five minutes. A low false-cue count does not mean high detection accuracy.

Camera-model accuracy

Not established by this experiment. The sensor model is synthetic and uncalibrated. There are no measured image-detector precision, recall or mAP results in this run, and these numbers do not validate the live camera connection.

What these results do and do not establish
  • Evidence for this map and scenario generator. The 200 untouched missions use random starts on connected sampled water and three synthetic visual conditions. Different vessel routes, sizes, weather or terrain can change the result.
  • Small placement search. Twelve candidate pairs and 24 validation scenarios provide a bounded comparison. More training time alone does not establish better performance; any new optimization needs fresh held-out testing.
  • Approximate geometry and dynamics. Grid spacing is about 135 m. Small obstructions, full aircraft dynamics, takeoff, wind, acceleration and realistic sensor timing are simplified or unmodeled.
  • Real perception needs its own evidence. Validate the live frame stream, camera-to-world projection, timestamp handling and detector on labeled video before applying these detection percentages to the running simulator.
  • Acquisition and sustained tracking are different objectives. The selected placement improves initial discovery but leaves many misses and worse conditional custody outside tower view.
