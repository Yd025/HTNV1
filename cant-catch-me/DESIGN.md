# Visual direction

Experience mode: the 3D strait is the interface's focal point. Show it immediately behind a lower-left start invitation; during play the interface recedes to a timer, pursuit warning, speed and geographic radar.

Palette: deep fjord #183d4c, cold water #388992, glacial sky #cadfe0, snow #edf4ee, rescue orange #f6a04d, radar mint #a8dcbd. Red is reserved for an active tag. Snow and water carry most of the viewport. Type is locally hosted IBM Plex Sans, with a bold, tightly set lower-case title and tabular numerals for changing measurements.

The deliberate visual gesture is an orange fishing vessel leaving a white wake in the actual rocky strait. UI controls borrow the clear silhouettes of a boat instrument panel without reproducing a military dashboard. Intro is left aligned; score is top center; geographic minimap is bottom right. Responsive touch controls sit at the lower edges; the intro simplifies on short screens. Keyboard focus is visible, reduced motion removes camera bob, pause is available by button and Escape, and loss states always expose retry.

The default gameplay view is now a close helm camera offset above and to starboard of the bridge. Keep the bow and nearby water visible for reading waves and the ground-projected drone shadow. Speed widens the lens slightly. The hull follows four wave samples with damped heave, pitch and roll, combined with its response to throttle and turning; both cameras share its interpolated position. Gradual engine and rudder response, momentum, turning drag and sideways slip give steering a physical feel. Camera sway is optional and respects reduced-motion settings. C toggles the retained chase view; hold Space or toggle the look-back button to see the approach behind the boat. Camera cuts happen immediately for deliberate view changes; no long fly-in consumes the opening seconds.

Loading extends the same visual system: broad flowing river bands, fine current traces, and an orange boat with a moving wake. Three labeled stages reflect module, asset and scene readiness; reduced motion holds the illustration still. The guide uses a compact key grid and short rules, available from the start screen and pause menu.

Orange circular gates with twin chevrons identify speed pickups in the world and on radar. A countdown and thin duration bar join the speed instrument during a boost. The current stretch and traveled distance sit in normal flow above the radar. A brief new-patrol notice marks each transition and yields to tower contact or an active tag. Give the score a dark fjord backing so all timer text remains legible against bright water and sky.

Use a compact amber warning for actual tower contact, naming T1, T2, or both. Keep it nonmodal and away from driving controls. It disappears when the tower sighting ends; searching alone must not show it. The general pursuit status explains that drones accelerate during any live sensor contact. Reserve red for the uninterrupted tag countdown so the warning levels remain distinct.

Keep the radar centered on the white boat arrow while the chart moves beneath it. Cache an oversized terrain image in 250-metre steps to preserve smooth movement between updates. Coverage sectors, tower labels, D1/D2, the cyan scout and boost markers remain available in every stretch. The 3D terrain follows with nine bounded tiles, and fresh patrol models appear at their new locations without a flight interpolation across the whole map. Boat motion and the camera remain continuous through the transition.
